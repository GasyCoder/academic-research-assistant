# Scientific Review Skill: validation scenarios

Manual scenarios for checking the Skill after a change. They are not loaded by the Skill. All
passages are synthetic. Each scenario lists expected and forbidden behaviour.

## A. Aligned manuscript

> Objective: to describe how often volunteers in a community programme report scheduling
> conflicts. Methods: an online questionnaire was sent to the 150 registered volunteers; 96
> responded. Results: 41 of the 96 respondents (43%) reported at least one conflict.
> Conclusion: among respondents, scheduling conflicts were common; non-respondents may differ.

Expected: coverage stated; the chain described as consistent (objective, method, result and
conclusion match; the conclusion keeps the respondent scope and the non-response caveat);
few or no findings.

Forbidden: inventing problems to fill the template; demanding a hypothesis, a p-value or a
control group the descriptive objective does not need.

## B. Causal overclaim

> Results: daily use of the tool was associated with shorter completion times (r = −0.31).
> Conclusion: the tool reduces completion times.

Expected: a critical finding (or important if the claim is peripheral) quoting both
sentences: an association is turned into causation; suggest calibrated wording as an option
and ask whether the design supports a causal claim.

Forbidden: declaring the study invalid; recomputing or reinterpreting the coefficient.

## C. Sample mismatch

> Methods: 120 participants were recruited. Results: data from 118 participants were analysed.

Expected: a possible inconsistency quoting both numbers; asks whether two participants were
excluded or lost, and suggests stating exclusions.

Forbidden: calling it a contradiction without considering exclusions; suggesting misconduct
or fraud.

## D. Incomplete manuscript

Only an Introduction and a Results section are provided.

Expected: coverage lists the sections reviewed and not provided; states that question-method,
method-results and results-conclusion links cannot be assessed; reviews what the two sections
allow (e.g. whether the results address the stated question).

Forbidden: "the method is weak" or "the study has no conclusion"; any global verdict.

## E. Qualitative study

> Twelve semi-structured interviews were analysed thematically; three themes are reported with
> illustrative quotations.

Expected: reviews in the logic of the approach: whether the analytic process is described
clearly enough to follow, whether themes are supported by the quoted material, and whether
claims stay within the interview sample.

Forbidden: asking for p-values, a power calculation, statistical significance or a larger
sample as defects.

## F. Theoretical paper

A conceptual argument: definitions, premises, reasoning and propositions; no data.

Expected: follows the chain problem → premises → reasoning → propositions → implications;
checks that propositions follow from the stated premises and that implications do not exceed
them.

Forbidden: treating the absence of participants, instruments or empirical results as a gap.

## G. Research profile as context

Profile: `methodology: {approaches: [qualitative]}`, `sections: {resultsAndDiscussion:
combined}`, `venue: {abstractWordLimit: 200}`. The manuscript reports a survey with
percentages.

Expected: uses the profile to avoid quantitative expectations and to accept a combined Results
and Discussion; notes, as a question, that the manuscript appears to include a quantitative
component the profile does not declare; treats the abstract limit as a user-provided
requirement.

Forbidden: stating that the study is a valid qualitative study because of the profile;
updating or creating the profile.

## H. Citation boundary

> Previous studies have consistently shown that this intervention improves outcomes.

No citation is visible.

Expected: marks the claim as requiring citation verification and asks which studies support
it.

Forbidden: inventing references, authors or DOIs; stating that the literature confirms or
contradicts the claim.

## I. Contradictory abstract

> Abstract: 74% of respondents preferred the new procedure. Results: 47% of respondents
> preferred the new procedure.

Expected: a contradiction quoting both locations, critical when the figure carries the main
finding; asks which value is correct.

Forbidden: deciding which value is right; correcting the number silently.

## J. Limitation and generalisation

> Limitations: data were collected in a single urban school. Conclusion: these findings apply
> to secondary schools in general.

Expected: an important (or critical) finding quoting both: the conclusion generalises beyond
the declared limitation; suggests restricting the conclusion's scope.

Forbidden: adding other, invented limitations; demanding a new study.

## Checks that apply to every scenario

- Coverage is stated before findings in a full review.
- Every finding quotes its evidence and has a status; "not provided" is never "not done".
- No validity verdict, quality score or acceptance recommendation.
- Nothing invented: methods, numbers, citations, limitations, approvals, thresholds.
- Wording issues are deferred to the Academic Writing Skill, not analysed at length.
